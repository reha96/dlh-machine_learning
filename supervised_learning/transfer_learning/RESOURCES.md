## Regularization — Data Augmentation and Transfer Learning — Alexander Jung

URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8-bLOSTBgG8  ·  Date: 2021-11-08 (YouTube upload)  ·  Status: summary

A ~38-minute lecture from a "Deep Learning with Python" course that frames nearly all of applied deep learning as regularization, then studies two popular regularizers in depth: data augmentation and transfer learning. The through-line is capacity control: deep nets memorize small datasets, so you either synthesize more training variety (augmentation) or import features learned on a large source task (transfer) instead of fitting millions of random weights from scratch. Transfer learning is presented as reuse of early-layer generic detectors plus adaptation of late layers.

- Thesis: you rarely run an unregularized deep net; augmentation and transfer are two of the most popular controls.
- Augmentation = cheap synthetic diversity (flips, rotations, crops) that slows memorization.
- Transfer = parameter import: early generic features carry over, late task-specific layers get replaced.
- Task link: conceptual backing for task 3 (augmentation) and the freeze/unfreeze choices in tasks 0-2.

## Transfer learning notes — CS231n

URL: https://cs231n.github.io/transfer-learning/  ·  Date: 2026-05-14 (page last modified)  ·  Status: summary

Stanford's course notes argue almost nobody trains a ConvNet from scratch because few datasets are big enough; instead you pretrain on ImageNet-scale data and either reuse the net as a fixed feature extractor (chop the 1000-way head, train a linear SVM/softmax on the CNN codes, e.g. a 4096-D AlexNet vector, keeping the ReLU thresholding the codes were trained with) or fine-tune by continuing backprop into some or all pretrained layers. The famous decision table crosses dataset size with similarity to the source: small-plus-similar means train a linear head on top-layer codes; large-plus-similar means fine-tune everything; small-plus-different means train the linear head from earlier, more generic activations; large-plus-different means you may train from scratch but initializing from pretrained weights still usually wins. Practical notes cover running pretrained conv/pool stacks on new image sizes, converting FC layers to convolutions, and using a smaller LR for the pretrained weights than for the fresh head.

- Fixed extractor vs fine-tuning vs released checkpoints (the Caffe Model Zoo era) is the original three-way split.
- Early layers hold generic edge/blob detectors; late layers hold source-specific class details (the dog-breed example).
- Four-case table (size x similarity) is the single most-quoted TL decision rule; memorize it for the objectives.
- CNN codes must stay ReLU'd if they were ReLU'd during pretraining.
- Small LR for pretrained weights, larger LR for the new head — the same warning the Keras guide repeats.
- Task link: the theory behind every task 0-2 choice (what to freeze, where to cut, which LR).

## Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition (ResNet paper)

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385  ·  Date: 2015-12-10 (arXiv submission)  ·  Status: summary

He et al. reformulate very deep layers as learning residual functions relative to their inputs instead of unreferenced mappings, adding identity skip connections so a solver can default a block to the identity rather than degrading. The evidence is a 152-layer ImageNet net — 8x deeper than VGG yet lower complexity — whose ensemble reached 3.57% test error and won ILSVRC 2015 classification (plus detection, localization, COCO detection and segmentation with the same backbone), with CIFAR-10 ablations up to 1000 layers showing optimization, not overfitting, was the depth barrier.

- Residual block: fit F(x) = H(x) − x and add x back, so identity is the easy default.
- Depth result: 152 layers beat VGG-era depth while staying cheaper; degradation solved as optimization failure.
- Competition record: ILSVRC 2015 classification/detection/localization plus COCO detection/segmentation.
- Task link: why ResNet50 is a first-class backbone choice for task 0 alongside MobileNetV2; skip-connection intuition for "which layers to unfreeze" discussions.

## EfficientNet: Rethinking Model Scaling for Convolutional Neural Networks

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11946  ·  Date: 2019-05-28 (arXiv submission, v1)  ·  Status: summary

Tan and Le show that scaling only one of depth, width or resolution gives diminishing returns, and propose compound scaling: grow all three together under one coefficient after a small neural-architecture search finds the B0 baseline. The family then spans B0 to B7, with B7 hitting 84.3% ImageNet top-1 while roughly 8x smaller and 6x faster than the best prior ConvNet, and the same models transfer strongly to CIFAR-100, Flowers and three more transfer datasets with an order of magnitude fewer parameters.

- Compound coefficient: depth, width and resolution scale jointly, not independently.
- B0 from NAS, B1-B7 from the scaling rule — a family, not one model.
- Efficiency claim with numbers: 84.3% top-1, ~8.4x smaller, ~6.1x faster inference.
- Transfer result: state of the art on five transfer datasets with far fewer parameters.
- Task link: the "how to choose a pretrained model" objective — EfficientNetB0 is the accuracy-per-parameter pick when MobileNetV2 is too small.

## Data augmentation for transfer learning — Perez & Wang effectiveness study

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04621  ·  Date: 2017-12-13 (arXiv submission)  ·  Status: summary

Perez and Wang deliberately restrict themselves to a small ImageNet subset and rank augmentation strategies head to head: classic transforms (crop, rotate, flip) remain among the most successful, GAN-synthesized styles are tried as an exotic alternative, and a learned "neural augmentation" policy is proposed that lets a net discover its own best augmentations. The takeaway for transfer work is that when the target set is tiny, augmentation choice is a first-order performance lever, not an afterthought — traditional transforms first, learned policies as the research direction, with per-dataset variance honestly reported.

- Tiny-ImageNet-subset protocol mirrors the transfer-learning small-data regime directly.
- Ranking: traditional transforms win reliably; GAN styles and neural augmentation are experimental.
- Neural augmentation = learn the augmentation policy jointly with the classifier.

## Feature extraction vs fine-tuning — Bosco (Data Reply)

URL: https://medium.com/data-reply-it-datatech/transfer-learning-feature-extraction-and-fine-tuning-db7d82767992  ·  Date: 2024-03-26 (publication)  ·  Status: summary

Bosco's five-minute comparison (fully readable, no paywall on 2026-09-23) restates the cs231n rule in decision form: both techniques reuse a model pretrained on a large source task because early layers hold generic detectors and late layers hold source-specific details, but feature extraction freezes the base and trains only a new classifier (cheap, best for small or dissimilar-target data, reading higher generic or lower specific layers accordingly, with overfitting as the failure mode) while fine-tuning unfreezes the top base layers and jointly trains everything at extra compute cost (best for larger target sets similar to the source, with dropout and early stopping against overfitting). It closes by naming the backbones (VGG, ResNet, GoogLeNet on ImageNet's 14M images / 20k classes) and positioning transfer as a step toward continuous learning.

- One-paragraph decision rule: small data or limited compute goes extraction; larger similar data goes fine-tuning.
- Layer-depth rule: similar target reads higher-layer features, dissimilar target reads lower-layer features.
- Cost contrast: extraction trains only the head; fine-tuning backprops through the whole stack.
- Regularization named: dropout and early stopping for the fine-tuning overfit risk.
- Task link: the objectives' vocabulary sheet (feature reuse, frozen vs unfrozen, general-vs-specific layers) in one page.

## Quiz Hooks

- Transfer learning — reuse ImageNet-scale features for a small target task instead of training from scratch.
- Feature extraction — freeze the base, train only a new head on CNN codes.
- Fine-tuning — unfreeze some or all of the base and retrain end to end at a very low LR.
- CNN codes — ReLU'd activation vectors from a headless pretrained net (e.g. 4096-D AlexNet) feeding a linear classifier.
- Generic early vs specific late layers — edges and blobs first, source-class details last (dog-breed example).
- Size-x-similarity table — small+similar: linear head on top codes; small+different: head on earlier activations; large: fine-tune.
- trainable=False — moves weights to non-trainable; recurses into sublayers; recompile or changes are ignored.
- training=False — keep BatchNorm in inference mode when calling a frozen or fine-tuned base.
- GlobalAveragePooling2D head — the task-0 top model: pool the frozen base output, then classify.
- Compound scaling — EfficientNet scales depth, width and resolution jointly under one coefficient.
- Residual connection — learn F(x) = H(x) − x and add x back so identity is the default.
- Data augmentation — synthetic diversity (crop, flip, rotate) as the small-data regularizer.
